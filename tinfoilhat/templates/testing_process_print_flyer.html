TINFOIL HAT COMPETITION

HOW TESTING WORKS

From scan to score — what happens when you put on the hat

01

50 FREQUENCIES ARE SELECTED

The system scans across 50 frequencies from 2 MHz to 5,900 MHz. These include real-world signals you encounter every day: FM radio, cellular bands, WiFi (2.4 & 5 GHz), Bluetooth, GPS, and more. The rest are evenly spaced across the spectrum to catch anything in between.

02

BASELINE SCAN — NO HAT

First, the HackRF SDR measures the ambient RF power at each frequency with no hat present. This is the baseline — the natural background signal level the hat will be tested against. Multiple readings are taken per frequency and averaged for stability.

03

HAT SCAN — WITH HAT

The hat is placed on the mannequin head (which contains the receiving antenna). The same 50 frequencies are measured again. A well-shielded hat will show lower power readings — the signal couldn't get through.

ATTENUATION (dB) = BASELINE − HAT READING

Positive = hat blocked signal   |   Negative = hat reflected or amplified signal (bad)

04

YOUR SCORE IS CALCULATED

Attenuation is calculated at each valid frequency, then averaged across all 50 frequencies. That average — in dB — is your score. Higher is better. Scores are also broken down by frequency band:

Band Range Includes
HF2 – 30 MHzShortwave, CB radio
VHF30 – 300 MHzFM radio, weather, air traffic
UHF300 MHz – 3 GHzCellular, WiFi 2.4 GHz, GPS, Bluetooth
SHF3 – 5.9 GHzWiFi 5 GHz, 5G mid-band
05

BEST SCORE IS TRACKED

Contestants can run multiple tests. Only your best score per hat type (Classic or Hybrid) appears on the leaderboard. Classic and Hybrid categories are ranked separately.

NOTE Negative attenuation is real — foil can reflect signals back and create resonance that briefly amplifies certain frequencies. It drags your average down. Smooth, gapless coverage across the whole head avoids this.
